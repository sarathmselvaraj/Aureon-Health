Disability Income Insurance: Protecting Your Household's Most Valuable Asset

By Rachel Adams (Disability & Income Protection Lead) • Sept 9, 2026 • 5 Min Read
The 1-in-4 Statistic:

According to the Social Security Administration, more than 25% of today's 20-year-olds will experience a disabling condition that keeps them out of work for a year or more before reaching retirement age. Most disabilities are caused by illness (arthritis, cancer, back injuries, depression) rather than dramatic accidents.

Your Earning Power vs. Your Possessions

Most people diligently insure their home ($500k value) and their automobile ($40k value). Yet, a 35-year-old professional earning $100,000 annually will generate $3,000,000+ in gross income over their remaining working career. Your ability to earn an income is far and away your most valuable financial asset.

Critical Clause: "Own-Occupation" vs. "Any-Occupation"

The single most important policy definition is the standard of disability required to trigger benefit checks:

Policy Definition How It Works Our Recommendation
True Own-Occupation Pays full monthly benefits if you cannot perform the material duties of your specific specialty—even if you work in another profession. Essential for surgeons, lawyers, engineers, and high earners
Any-Occupation Only pays benefits if you are unable to perform any gainful occupation suitable by your education or experience. Avoid if possible; very difficult to qualify for payouts

Selecting the Right Elimination Period

The Elimination Period functions like a deductible in time—it represents how many days you must wait between becoming disabled and when benefits begin. Choosing a 90-day or 180-day waiting period instead of 30 days can reduce your monthly policy premium by up to 40%, allowing you to self-insure the initial months with your emergency savings.

"If you had a machine in your basement that printed $8,000 in cash every month, you would insure it immediately. You are that machine."
